Edge AI Isn't a Cloud Story. It's a Cabinet Story.
RK3588 industrial edge computer 
Edge AI isn't a cloud story anymore. It's a cabinet story.

A single vision inspection station streams more data than most factory links can afford to ship. Push it all to the cloud and you get bandwidth bills, latency you can't control, and a hard dependency on a connection that fails at the worst moment.

The fix: process data where it's born. Our fanless industrial edge computer, built on the Rockchip RK3588, sits right next to the machines and makes the call on the spot.

The specs that actually matter in the field:

Octa-core RK3588 (4× Cortex-A76 + 4× A55, 2.0 GHz) with a 6 TOPS NPU — defect detection, OCR and object detection run locally, inference in tens of milliseconds
8K media engine — 8K@30 H.264 / 8K@60 VP9 / 4K@60 AV1 decode, 8K@30 H.265 encode: ingest multiple camera streams, run analytics, transcode — simultaneously
4× Gigabit Ethernet — one link upstream, the rest to PLCs, camera VLANs or redundant segments. No external switch
8 GB LPDDR4, 64 GB eMMC (up to 256 GB), SATA 3.0 for local buffering — an outage costs data points, not data
RS-232, USB 2.0 ×4, HDMI — the legacy instruments and HMIs in every real cabinet
Fanless aluminum chassis, 158 × 133 mm, 12 V / ~10 W, rated −40 °C to +85 °C
That last line is the whole product. No fan to seize after two years of dust. It runs in unheated winter cabinets and sealed summer enclosures alike. At the roadside, that's the difference between a product and a return shipment.

Fits: industrial IoT gateways, smart transportation (LPR, traffic analytics), edge vision inspection, compact NVR.

Edge wins not because it's cheaper than the cloud, but because it removes a dependency — bandwidth, latency and uptime become variables you control inside your own cabinet.
